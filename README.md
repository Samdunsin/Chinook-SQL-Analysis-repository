# Chinook-SQL-Analysis-repository

Basic SQL and database exploration
------------------- SELECT, WHERE, ORDER BY, DISTINCT and LIMIT.
1. How many customers are registered in the music store?
SQL concept: COUNT
SELECT COUNT(*) AS Total_Customers
FROM Customer;


What it answers: Returns the total number of customers registered in the database.

Business relevance: Helps establish the size of the store's customer base.
2. Which countries do the customers come from?
SQL concept: DISTINCT, ORDER BY
SELECT DISTINCT Country
FROM Customer
ORDER BY Country ASC;


What it answers: Returns a list of unique countries represented in the customer database.

Business relevance: Helps understand the geographical reach of the business.
3. Which 10 customers are located in the USA?
SQL concept: WHERE, ORDER BY, LIMIT
SELECT 
    FirstName,
    LastName,
    Country,
    City
FROM Customer
WHERE Country = 'USA'
ORDER BY LastName ASC
LIMIT 10;


What it answers: Returns 10 American customers, arranged alphabetically by surname.
Business relevance: Helps retrieve customer information for geographical segmentation.


4. How many invoices have been generated by the store?
SQL concept: COUNT
SELECT COUNT(*) AS Total_Invoices
FROM Invoice;


What it answers: Returns the total number of invoices recorded.
Business relevance: Provides a basic measure of transaction volume.

5. Which are the five most expensive tracks in the music store?
SQL concept: ORDER BY, LIMIT
SELECT 
    Name AS Track_Name,
    UnitPrice
FROM Track
ORDER BY UnitPrice DESC
LIMIT 5;
What it answers: Displays the five tracks with the highest listed unit prices.
Business relevance: Helps identify the most expensive products in the catalogue.

-----------Aggregation and business performance------------
Here, we move beyond retrieving records. We begin summarising data to answer business questions.

6. What is the total revenue generated by the music store?
SQL concept: SUM
SELECT 
    ROUND(SUM(Total), 2) AS Total_Revenue
FROM Invoice;
What it answers: Calculates the sum of all invoice totals.
Business relevance: Establishes the overall invoiced revenue recorded in the dataset.

7. Which countries generate the highest revenue?
SQL concept: GROUP BY, SUM, ORDER BY
SELECT 
    BillingCountry,
    ROUND(SUM(Total), 2) AS Total_Revenue
FROM Invoice
GROUP BY BillingCountry
ORDER BY Total_Revenue DESC;

What it answers: Calculates revenue for each billing country and ranks the countries from highest to lowest.
Business relevance: Helps identify the strongest geographical revenue markets.

8. Who are the top 10 customers by total spending?
SQL concept: INNER JOIN, GROUP BY, SUM
SELECT 
    c.CustomerId,
    c.FirstName,
    c.LastName,
    ROUND(SUM(i.Total), 2) AS Total_Spending
FROM Customer c
INNER JOIN Invoice i
    ON c.CustomerId = i.CustomerId
GROUP BY 
    c.CustomerId,
    c.FirstName,
    c.LastName
ORDER BY Total_Spending DESC
LIMIT 10;


What it answers: Identifies the 10 customers who have spent the most money.
Business relevance: Helps identify high-value customers who may deserve special retention attention.

9. Which music genres generate the highest sales revenue?
SQL concept: Multiple INNER JOINs, GROUP BY, SUM
SELECT 
    g.Name AS Genre,
    ROUND(SUM(il.UnitPrice * il.Quantity), 2) AS Total_Revenue
FROM Genre g
INNER JOIN Track t
    ON g.GenreId = t.GenreId
INNER JOIN InvoiceLine il
    ON t.TrackId = il.TrackId
GROUP BY g.GenreId, g.Name
ORDER BY Total_Revenue DESC;


What it answers: Calculates the revenue generated by each music genre.
Business relevance: Helps the business understand which music categories contribute most to revenue.

10. Which artists generate the highest revenue?
SQL concept: Multiple JOINs, GROUP BY, SUM
SELECT 
    ar.Name AS Artist,
    ROUND(SUM(il.UnitPrice * il.Quantity), 2) AS Total_Revenue
FROM Artist ar
INNER JOIN Album al
    ON ar.ArtistId = al.ArtistId
INNER JOIN Track t
    ON al.AlbumId = t.AlbumId
INNER JOIN InvoiceLine il
    ON t.TrackId = il.TrackId
GROUP BY ar.ArtistId, ar.Name
ORDER BY Total_Revenue DESC
LIMIT 10;


What it answers: Identifies the 10 artists whose tracks have generated the highest sales revenue.
Business relevance: Helps identify commercially successful artists in the music catalogue.

11. Which albums contain the highest number of tracks?
SQL concept: INNER JOIN, COUNT, GROUP BY
SELECT 
    al.Title AS Album,
    ar.Name AS Artist,
    COUNT(t.TrackId) AS Total_Tracks
FROM Album al
INNER JOIN Artist ar
    ON al.ArtistId = ar.ArtistId
INNER JOIN Track t
    ON al.AlbumId = t.AlbumId
GROUP BY 
    al.AlbumId,
    al.Title,
    ar.Name
ORDER BY Total_Tracks DESC
LIMIT 10;


What it answers: Identifies the 10 albums with the largest number of tracks.

Business relevance: Helps understand the size and distribution of the music catalogue.

12. Which employees are responsible for the highest number of customers?
SQL concept: LEFT JOIN, COUNT, GROUP BY
SELECT 
    e.EmployeeId,
    e.FirstName,
    e.LastName,
    COUNT(c.CustomerId) AS Total_Customers
FROM Employee e
LEFT JOIN Customer c
    ON e.EmployeeId = c.SupportRepId
GROUP BY 
    e.EmployeeId,
    e.FirstName,
    e.LastName
ORDER BY Total_Customers DESC;


What it answers: Counts the number of customers assigned to each employee.
Business relevance: Helps management understand customer allocation among support representatives.
13. Are there customers who have never made a purchase?
SQL concept: LEFT JOIN, IS NULL
SELECT 
    c.CustomerId,
    c.FirstName,
    c.LastName,
    c.Email
FROM Customer c
LEFT JOIN Invoice i
    ON c.CustomerId = i.CustomerId
WHERE i.InvoiceId IS NULL;


What it answers: Identifies customers who have no matching invoice records.
Business relevance: Helps identify registered customers who have not converted into purchasing customers.

14. Which tracks have never been purchased?
SQL concept: LEFT JOIN, IS NULL
SELECT 
    t.TrackId,
    t.Name AS Track_Name
FROM Track t
LEFT JOIN InvoiceLine il
    ON t.TrackId = il.TrackId
WHERE il.TrackId IS NULL;


What it answers: Identifies tracks that have never appeared in an invoice line.
Business relevance: Helps identify products with no recorded sales activity.

15. What is the average value of an invoice?
SQL concept: AVG
SELECT 
    ROUND(AVG(Total), 2) AS Average_Invoice_Value
FROM Invoice;


What it answers: Calculates the average value of all invoices.
Business relevance: Establishes the average transaction value, which can be used to monitor changes in customer spending.

-----------------Advanced SQL -----------------------
These questions introduce date functions, subqueries, conditional logic and Common Table Expressions.
16. What is the monthly revenue trend?
SQL concept: GROUP BY, DATE_FORMAT, SUM
SELECT 
    DATE_FORMAT(InvoiceDate, '%Y-%m') AS Sales_Month,
    ROUND(SUM(Total), 2) AS Monthly_Revenue
FROM Invoice
GROUP BY DATE_FORMAT(InvoiceDate, '%Y-%m')
ORDER BY Sales_Month ASC;


What it answers: Calculates the total revenue generated in each month, across the years represented in the dataset.

Business relevance: Helps identify periods of high and low sales activity.
17. Which year generated the highest revenue?
SQL concept: YEAR, GROUP BY, ORDER BY
SELECT 
    YEAR(InvoiceDate) AS Sales_Year,
    ROUND(SUM(Total), 2) AS Annual_Revenue
FROM Invoice
GROUP BY YEAR(InvoiceDate)
ORDER BY Annual_Revenue DESC;

What it answers: Calculates annual revenue and ranks the years from highest to lowest.
Business relevance: Helps management compare revenue performance across different years.

18. Which customers have spent more than the average customer?
SQL concept: Subqueries, GROUP BY, HAVING
SELECT 
    c.CustomerId,
    c.FirstName,
    c.LastName,
    ROUND(SUM(i.Total), 2) AS Total_Spending
FROM Customer c
INNER JOIN Invoice i
    ON c.CustomerId = i.CustomerId
GROUP BY 
    c.CustomerId,
    c.FirstName,
    c.LastName
HAVING SUM(i.Total) > (
    SELECT AVG(Customer_Spending)
    FROM (
        SELECT 
            SUM(Total) AS Customer_Spending
        FROM Invoice
        GROUP BY CustomerId
    ) AS CustomerTotals
)
ORDER BY Total_Spending DESC;


What it answers: Identifies customers whose total spending exceeds the average total spending per purchasing customer.
Business relevance: Helps identify above-average spending customers for targeted loyalty and retention initiatives.

19. How much revenue is generated by customers assigned to each employee?
SQL concept: Multiple JOINs, GROUP BY, SUM
SELECT 
    e.EmployeeId,
    e.FirstName,
    e.LastName,
    ROUND(SUM(i.Total), 2) AS Total_Revenue
FROM Employee e
INNER JOIN Customer c
    ON e.EmployeeId = c.SupportRepId
INNER JOIN Invoice i
    ON c.CustomerId = i.CustomerId
GROUP BY 
    e.EmployeeId,
    e.FirstName,
    e.LastName
ORDER BY Total_Revenue DESC;


What it answers: Calculates the total invoice revenue from customers assigned to each employee.
Business relevance: Provides a view of revenue associated with each employee's customer portfolio.

Important limitation: This is not necessarily revenue directly generated by an employee. It is revenue from their assigned customers. Do not use it as a standalone employee performance measure.

20. Which playlists contain the highest number of tracks?
SQL concept: CTE, JOIN, COUNT, ORDER BY
WITH PlaylistSummary AS (
    SELECT 
        p.PlaylistId,
        p.Name AS Playlist_Name,
        COUNT(pt.TrackId) AS Total_Tracks
    FROM Playlist p
    LEFT JOIN PlaylistTrack pt
        ON p.PlaylistId = pt.PlaylistId
    GROUP BY p.PlaylistId, p.Name
)

SELECT 
    Playlist_Name,
    Total_Tracks
FROM PlaylistSummary
ORDER BY Total_Track
